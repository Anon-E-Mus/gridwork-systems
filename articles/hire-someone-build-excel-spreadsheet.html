Where to hire someone to build a custom Excel spreadsheet (2026 rates)

Fiverr vs Upwork vs fixed-price specialists vs templates: honest 2026 comparison with real rates and how to brief a builder so you get what you pictured.

Bookkeeping & Systems3 min readGridwork Systems · Nov 2026
The gistFiverr vs Upwork vs fixed-price specialists vs templates: honest 2026 comparison with real rates and how to brief a builder so you get what you pictured.

You know exactly the spreadsheet you want. Tracking the fleet, costing the jobs, quoting the clients, whatever it is, you have described it three times to three people and nobody has built it yet. Here are your actual options in 2026, what they cost, and when each is the right call.

Option 1: Fiverr gigs ($15 to $100)

Search "excel spreadsheet" on Fiverr and you will find hundreds of gigs. The good ones deliver exactly what the tile says: a form, a dashboard, a tracker from your description. Best for small, well-defined jobs.

The honest limits: quality swings wildly, revisions are usually limited, and the person building it has usually never run a business on the tools they build. Communication happens through the platform, which slows everything. Check reviews for "understood my business", not just stars.

Option 2: Upwork freelancers ($30 to $150 per hour)

Higher ceiling. Serious Excel consultants live here, including ones who build financial models for funds. Best when the job is big, fuzzy, or needs to evolve over months.

The tradeoffs: hourly billing means scope creep costs you money, vetting takes real time, and the good ones are booked. For a one-off business tracker, paying $150 an hour to re-explain your business is a rough deal.

Option 4: Build it yourself from a template ($19 to $79)

If what you want is close to something that already exists, a prebuilt template beats custom. An invoice tracker, a job-costing system, a cashflow forecaster: these are solved problems. Custom work pays off when your workflow is genuinely unusual. We sell both, and we tell people to take the template when a template fits.

How to brief a spreadsheet builder (whoever you hire)

Write down the five questions the spreadsheet must answer. Who owes me money and how late? Which jobs made money? What does March look like if the big client pays on the 28th? A builder who asks about your questions before your formulas is the right one. Attach a photo of the paper or the ugly sheet you use now, that artifact is worth an hour of discovery.

Then agree on: what's included, how many revisions, the delivery format, and what happens if it is not right. Any professional will answer those four without flinching.

The honest bottom line

Under $50 and simple: Fiverr. Big or evolving: Upwork, budget accordingly. A defined business tool at a known price: a fixed-price specialist. Nearly a solved problem: buy the template and spend the savings on using it.
