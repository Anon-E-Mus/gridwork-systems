Excel vs QuickBooks for a 5-person business: honest math

A $200–540/year subscription that solves problems you may not have yet — versus a system where you see every number. The honest math.

Bookkeeping & Systems3 min readGridwork Systems · Nov 2026
The gistA $200–540/year subscription that solves problems you may not have yet — versus a system where you see every number. The honest math.

QuickBooks is genuinely good software. It's also a $200–540/year subscription that solves problems a five-person business may not have yet. Here's the honest math on when the spreadsheet stops being enough.

The case nobody makes honestly

Search "do I need QuickBooks?" and you'll find two kinds of answers: accountants saying yes (they receive cleaner files from you, on your dime), and spreadsheet enthusiasts saying no (romantics). Neither starts from the actual math. So:

QuickBooks Online, Simple Start tier (2026 pricing): roughly $19–35/month depending on the current promotion — call it $230–420/year, every year, rising as tiers climb. Add payroll processing and it's multiples more.

A well-built spreadsheet system: one-time cost of a dinner, or free — and about 15 focused minutes a week of your attention.

That's the money. The real question is what each buys, and at what headcount it flips.

What QuickBooks genuinely does better

Honesty first:

  • Bank feeds and auto-categorization. Transactions pull in and mostly sort themselves. In a spreadsheet you type them. At high transaction volume this is the feature.
  • Payroll. If you have employees, payroll software earns its price on the first filing deadline it handles for you. This is the single strongest reason to subscribe.
  • Reconciliation as a formal, audited process.
  • The accountant handshake. Your accountant opens a .QBO file and everything is where they expect.

The five honest flip points

Move to accounting software when — not before — any of these becomes true:

1. You hire. Payroll alone justifies the subscription.

2. Transaction volume passes ~100/month. Typing now costs real hours.

3. You incorporate and your accountant insists. Their time is billed hourly; clean software files cut their bill more than the subscription costs.

4. Inventory or multi-currency enters the picture.

5. You stop doing the books yourself and hand them to a bookkeeper — they'll be faster in software they know.

A five-person service business with straightforward clients, no payroll complexity beyond a payroll bureau, and a disciplined weekly habit? The spreadsheet is not the compromise option. It's the option where you understand your own numbers.

The migration tax — and how to avoid paying it twice

The quiet cost of "we'll just start on spreadsheets" isn't the spreadsheet; it's doing it in three disconnected files that don't map to anything. The fix: run the spreadsheet like an accounting system from day one — sequential invoice numbers, consistent categories, one register per concern, month boundaries respected. Do that, and the day you migrate (if you ever do), your accountant imports clean lists and the move costs an afternoon. Don't, and you're paying someone to reverse-engineer chaos.
