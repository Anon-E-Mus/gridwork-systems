Independent contractor expenses spreadsheet: the 12 categories that survive tax season

1099 contractors: the twelve Schedule C categories, one row per payment, receipt photo the same day. The sheet you'll actually keep, plus the tax set-aside habit.

Bookkeeping & Systems2 min readGridwork Systems · Nov 2026

If you drive your own truck, buy your own tools, and get 1099s instead of paychecks, your expense spreadsheet is not admin. It is the difference between keeping the money you earned and discovering in April that you can't prove any of it. Here is the layout contractors actually stick with.

Why most contractor expense sheets fail by February

They die of ambition. Forty categories, sub-columns for vehicles and per-diems, a mileage tab that assumes you'll log every trip. Nobody keeps that up alongside a full workload. The sheet that survives has twelve categories, one row per payment, and a photo of the receipt taken the same day.

The twelve categories (US 1099 Schedule C headings)

1. Advertising - listings, signs, business cards

2. Car and truck - fuel, repairs, insurance, parking (or take the mileage rate instead, 67 cents a mile in 2024 and indexed after - pick one method per vehicle and stay consistent)

3. Contract labor - the helper you paid in cash, yes it's deductible, no you can't prove it without their info

4. Insurance - liability, tools, vehicle commercial policies

5. Interest - truck loan, equipment financing

6. Legal and professional - the accountant, permits pulled by others

7. Office and supplies - fasteners, blades, blades, blades

8. Rent or lease - shop, storage, equipment rentals

9. Repairs and maintenance - tools and equipment

10. Taxes and licenses - city permits, state renewals

11. Travel and meals - out-of-area jobs, 50 percent of meals while traveling

12. Utilities - shop electricity, the phone plan you actually use for scheduling

Twelve. Every payment fits one row: date, vendor, amount, category, one-line note. That's the whole sheet. The discipline is taking the receipt photo the same day, because reconstructing three months of paper in March is how people start hating the work.

First, the tax set-aside row: move 25 to 30 percent of every payment into a separate account the day it lands. Contractors skip this and then owe a surprise in April. Second, the quarter check: 30 minutes every quarter, total by category, compare against the same quarter last year. That's also the sheet your accountant wants, and it turns tax prep from a project into a printout.
