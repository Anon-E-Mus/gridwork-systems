MTD for self assessment: the spreadsheet route explained

From April 2026 the self-employed must file quarterly. The legal spreadsheet route costs £29 plus a £0-30 bridge - not a £360/year subscription. Here's exactly how it works.

From 6 April 2026, self-employed people and landlords with gross income over £50,000 must keep digital records and send HMRC a quarterly update. The software industry would like you to believe that means a subscription. It doesn't. The spreadsheet route is legal, cheaper, and for most sole traders, better: here's exactly how it works.

What actually changes in April 2026

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax Self Assessment (MTD ITSA) replaces the annual return with two obligations: keep your income and expense records digitally (not in a shoebox, not in a paper ledger), and send HMRC a summary every quarter, plus one final declaration at the tax year end.

The thresholds arrive in waves: £50,000 gross income first (April 2026), then £30,000 (April 2027), then £20k is planned for 2028. If you're a sole trader or landlord above the line, this is your year to get set up. HMRC's first year has a soft-landing attitude to penalties, but the records obligation starts on day one.

The two legal routes

Route one: subscribe to MTD-compatible accounting software. It files everything for you and costs roughly £120–£360 a year, forever.

Route two: keep your records in a spreadsheet and submit through bridging software that connects the spreadsheet's numbers to HMRC's API. Bridging tools include VitalTax (an Excel add-in, about £30 a year), My Tax Digital (free core), and TapTax or 123 Sheets (about £20 a year). The spreadsheet does the record-keeping; the bridge does the submitting. Both halves are MTD-legal.

One thing to be clear about, because some template sellers fudge it: a spreadsheet alone cannot submit anything to HMRC. The bridge step is not optional. Anyone selling you a "MTD-compatible spreadsheet" without naming the bridge is selling you half a solution.

Nothing exotic: date, amount, category for every income and expense line, held digitally. The expense categories follow the self-employment allowable headings most sole traders already know: cost of goods sold, wages, car and travel (45p for the first 10,000 miles, 25p after), business premises, repairs, phone and internet, insurance, professional fees, interest, and the rest. Simplified expenses (mileage and home-office flat rate) remain available. Capital allowances, including the Annual Investment Allowance, sit alongside.

The 20-minute quarterly routine

Each MTD period runs: 6 April–5 July, 6 July–5 October, 6 October–5 January, 6 January–5 April. Updates are due by the 7th of August, November, February and May, with the final declaration by 31 January.

The routine, once set up: log income and expenses as they happen (a minute a day), check the quarter tab's totals at period end, enter them in your bridging tool, submit. Twenty minutes, four times a year, plus a calmer January than you've had in years.

The honest limits

The spreadsheet doesn't file to HMRC: the bridge does that, and it's a separate (small) tool. If your affairs involve VAT, payroll, or a limited company, you need proper accounting software and probably an accountant: this route is for sole traders and landlords. And if you're the kind of person who won't log a receipt for three months, no spreadsheet or subscription fixes that habit.

For everyone else: £29 once plus £0–£30 a year beats £120–£360 a year, every year, for the same compliance. That's the spreadsheet route.
